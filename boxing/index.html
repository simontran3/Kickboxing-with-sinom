Boxing or kickboxing

Boxing or kickboxing? The honest difference

Kickboxing is boxing with kicks and knees added. The punches are the same, and boxing fundamentals are the usual starting point.

01

The same as boxing

The jab, the cross, hooks and uppercuts. Slipping and blocking punches. Rounds on the pads. If you came to learn how to punch, this is where you start.

02

What kickboxing adds

Kicks and knees, taught alongside the punches rather than instead of them. The basic body kick comes first, and Simon builds from there at your pace.

03

Why that helps you

You train your legs as well as your hands, and you learn to check a leg kick, not only to slip a punch.

Why it works

Why people looking for boxing try kickboxing

Boxing is the better-known sport, so plenty of people search for it first. None of Simon's clients has asked to do boxing only.

01

Your hands come first

Boxing fundamentals are the usual starting point, so your first session normally opens with the jab and cross.

02

Nobody watching you learn

Private sessions are one on one or with a friend, so you can get things wrong without an audience.

03

Technique, then fitness

Simon watches every repetition and fixes it on the spot. The technique moves first, and the fitness follows it.

Your first hour

What happens on your free first session

Nobody is thrown in the deep end. The first session is mostly Simon finding your starting point, and you finding out that this is easier to start than it looks.

01

Introduction

Simon shows you around the gym. You warm up and stretch on the mats while you talk through your goals and anything sore he should know about.

02

Fundamentals

Stance, guard and your first punches, from the ground up. Simon makes no assumptions about your experience, and he is very patient.

03

Pads, at your pace

Then you hit pads for as long as it feels good, and stop whenever you want. Simon's promise is that you will also land a roundhouse kick before the session ends.

04

Cool-down and stretching

Every session ends with yoga-inspired stretches that relax your muscles and bring your heart rate back down.

After that

What you learn, and roughly when

The first block is built on the punches: the jab and cross, with stance, guard and footwork underneath them. Nothing new gets added until the thing underneath it is solid.

01

Weeks 1 to 4

Stance, guard, footwork, jab, cross, and the basic body kick. The aim is a straight punch you can throw without thinking about it, and the ability to move without crossing your feet.

02

Weeks 5 to 8

Hooks, uppercuts, and different variations of kicks: teeps, low outside kicks, inside low kicks, and knees. Defence starts here too, so you learn to check a leg kick, slip, and block, rather than only throwing.

03

Weeks 9 to 12

Combinations, longer rounds, and reading what is coming back at you. This is where you'll also learn how to defend & counter back.

04

Beyond twelve weeks

Conditioning gets sharper, rounds get longer, and you start flowing like a kickboxer should. Some clients stay technical, some want the fitness, and some want to progress to sparring. All are fine and possible.

The honest answers

The questions people ask before booking

Is this a boxing class

It is kickboxing, and boxing is the base of it. Boxing fundamentals are the usual starting point, and kicks are taught alongside them.

What if I have never thrown a punch

Most people who book have not. There is no assumed knowledge in any of it, and Simon sets the pace off what you can do on the day.

Do I need to be fit before I start

No. Fitness is the thing you build here, not the thing you bring. Simon slows it down the moment you ask.

Do I need my own gloves

No. Gloves, wraps, pads, bags, and floor mats are all at the Lidcombe gym.

Do I have to spar

No. Sparring is optional and always will be. Plenty of clients train for years on pads and bags without ever taking a shot back.

Can I train with a friend

Yes. A semi private session is the same coaching shared between two of you, for a full hour.

Where is the gym
